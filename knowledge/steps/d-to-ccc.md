# Step: D → CCC

This is by far the thinnest-evidenced step in the entire ladder. The brief asks for this jump to carry the strictest requirements of all — the research supports that direction in spirit, but we were not able to find corroborated pilot-readiness criteria specific to CCC wings themselves. Most of what exists concerns *competition entry*, which is a related but distinct gate.

## What CCC actually is (recap from classification-overview.md)

CCC is not a passive-safety class like A-D. It's a FAI/CIVL competition-equipment specification: aspect ratio capped at 7.9, speed-bar travel capped at 18cm, no mid-span concave sections, and a somewhat relaxed (3-second rather than 5-second) reaction-time allowance in testing, with some of the most demanding EN-D test manoeuvres removed altogether. See [FAI Sporting Code Section 7G](https://www.fai.org/sites/default/files/civl/documents/sporting_code_s7_g_-_ccc_paragliders_requirements_2020_1.0_0.pdf) (official) and [FAI's history of serial vs competition class](https://www.fai.org/news/brief-history-serial-vs-competition-class-paragliders) (official).

## Hours / experience

- [richiesadventures.com — Two Liner Progression Tips](https://richiesadventures.com/2019/08/19/two-liner-progression-tips/) (industry/enthusiast) quotes another pilot's personal rule of thumb — "at least 300 hours" before considering a CCC wing — but this is a single secondhand opinion in a personal blog post, not corroborated anywhere else we found. We would not want the app to treat this as a reliable number.
- No official or manufacturer source we found gives an hours threshold specific to CCC wings themselves (as opposed to competition entry — see below).

## Competition-entry gating (a related but separate system)

- [US Paragliding Competitions — Pilot Qualifications](https://www.usparaglidingcompetitions.com/pilot-qualifications/) (official/industry, national comp series): to compete at all requires a USHPA P4 "Advanced Pilot" rating or an international licence with IPPI card level 4. Within competitions, there's a "Sports Class" capped at EN-C/DHV2-or-lower equipment, and a separate "Overall Class" that allows anything up to CCC. Notably, a wing-control/SIV course is described as "highly recommended," not mandatory, even for entry to the CCC-eligible Overall Class — a clear, official-adjacent data point in the SIV debate (see `disagreements.md`).
- [FAI Sporting Code Section 7G](https://www.fai.org/sites/default/files/civl/documents/sporting_code_s7_g_-_ccc_paragliders_requirements_2020_1.0_0.pdf) (official) governs the wing, not the pilot — it has nothing to say about pilot readiness.

**Key finding:** the official/competition system gates CCC access through *pilot rating and competition licensing* (P4/IPPI-4), not through a wing-specific hours or skills test. This is structurally different from every earlier step in the ladder, where (informal) hours/skills criteria were the main gating logic.

## Skills

- [Dust of the Universe](https://ziadbassil.blogspot.com/) (expert/independent tester) and industry commentary on serial-vs-CCC wings note that EN-D serial-class wings "fly nearly as much" as CCC wings now, with CCC's edge concentrated in high-speed glide performance — implying the skill gap between a well-flown EN-D and a CCC wing may be smaller in practice than the official separation suggests. This is a single independent-tester's comparative view, not a formal study.
- [Flybubble — Understanding CIVL Competition Class (CCC)](https://flybubble.com/blogs/blog/ccc) (industry) focuses entirely on the technical specification and design implications (narrow tips, increased cravat risk from the tapering) and does not address pilot skill prerequisites at all.

## Conditions flown / collapse handling / psychological factors

No source gave criteria specific to CCC wings beyond what's already covered for EN-D and 2-liners generally (see `high-c-to-d.md` and `low-c-to-high-c.md`). It's reasonable to assume everything that applies to a 2-liner EN-D wing applies at least as strongly to a CCC wing, but we have no direct evidence for anything *additional* that CCC specifically demands of a pilot, beyond the competition-licensing requirement.

**Checked specifically, Oct 2026 (owner-prompted gap-check):** strongest-thermal-survived-without-collapse, XC distance and site/terrain variety were all checked for this step specifically and none were found. This is a genuine gap, consistent with the rest of this step's thinness — the only even loosely-relevant material is the P4/IPPI-4 competition-licensing requirement already noted above, which says nothing about distance, site count or conditions survived.

**Speed bar, ground handling and motivation (checked specifically Oct 2026, genuine gap confirmed):** [the FAI CCC specification](https://www.fai.org/sites/default/files/civl/documents/sporting_code_s7_g_-_ccc_paragliders_requirements_2020_1.0_0.pdf) (official, already cited above) caps speedbar travel at 18cm as a wing-design constraint, but this is a technical limit on the equipment, not pilot-skill guidance — we found no source discussing how a pilot's speed-bar technique should differ on a CCC wing specifically, beyond what's already covered for EN-D/2-liners generally. Equally, no source discusses ground-handling expectations at CCC level distinct from EN-D, and no source discusses upgrade motivation specific to a D→CCC decision — the closest material (Ospina's cautionary story, the Head Game's ambition-vs-preparation framing) is general to high-performance wings, not CCC-specific. Given how small and specialised the CCC-flying population is, this plausibly reflects a real absence of published guidance rather than a search failure — but we flag it as an honest gap rather than stretching the EN-D material to cover it silently.

## Red flags

None found specific to this step beyond the general pattern (carried over from D) of ambition outrunning currency and SIV fluency.

## SIV

Same as the D step, but with one additional, genuinely official-adjacent data point: even a competition body that requires a P4/IPPI-4 rating to enter CCC-eligible competition only "highly recommends," rather than mandates, an SIV/wing-control course. This is a meaningful counterweight to the "SIV should be near-mandatory at the top of the ladder" framing found elsewhere, and supports keeping SIV non-gating throughout, including at CCC.

## Source-quality note and explicit gap

This step is thin by a wide margin. (Oct 2026 note: we specifically checked Greg Hamerton's published material — a former PWC competitor and instructor — for CCC-specific pilot-readiness guidance, since he's exactly the kind of voice who might have it. We did not find anything addressing CCC wings specifically, as opposed to general competition/high-performance flying; his relevant material is used in the earlier steps instead. This remains a genuine gap, not something we're papering over.) We recommend the app:
- Treat CCC readiness primarily through the lens of **competition licensing and rating** (e.g. asking whether the pilot already holds or is working toward a recognised advanced/competition pilot rating) rather than inventing a precise hours number not supported by sources.
- Avoid stating any specific hours figure for this step with confidence — the only number we found (300 hours) is a single unverified secondhand opinion.
- Require an explicit instructor/competition-mentor consultation recommendation at this step more heavily than at any other, given how little independent criteria exists to score against.
